# E8 — three application workloads (WP12, research design E8) — pre-registration

Committed and pushed BEFORE the traces are generated and before any harness runs.
- Base: main 301effc (v9.2). Plan: `work/plans/NEXT_ROUND_PLAN_20261013.md` WP12 (PR #61).
- **Traces: deterministic only** (user decision, 2026-10-15). No public traces and no real LLM agents. Every trace is generated by `gen_traces.mjs` from a frozen distribution and a seed.
  - The paper must say that the workloads are self-generated and are **not** an authoritative benchmark.
  - No claim is made about prompt injection: the system only bounds what a compromised or misbehaving agent can authorize.
- **Scope: real authorization layer.** Real v9.2 proofs, real ZkDacGateV9, ZkDacRevocations and ZkDacRegistry, real budget transfer / mint / revoke proofs, on anvil.
- **Scope: the applications are local stand-ins.**
  - A1's service is a local HTTP gateway.
  - A2's asset is a test ERC-20 without value.
  - A3's task is a simulated procurement.

  This boundary is reported.

## How the protocol expresses each restriction
| restriction | mechanism |
|---|---|
| resource / tool / category | a **method selector** per resource; the gate's `requiredCap[method]` is a capability bit; the circuit enforces req_cap ⊆ the holder certificate's cap ⊆ every ancestor's cap |
| method | as above (the bit differs per method) |
| time window | certificate expiry per hop (circuit: now ≤ expiry) and the gate's clock check |
| number of calls / spend | budget notes: every call spends its amount; a note is single-use (budget nullifier) |
| revocation / permission change | ZkDacRevocations (window Δ = 30 s). A permission change = issue a narrower certificate, then revoke the old edge. |

## Applications (one fresh world per seed)
**A1 — service access (tool gateway).**
- An operator O delegates to 3 agents. Each agent has a certificate for a subset of 4 tools (`search`, `fetch`, `compute`, `admin`): one method and one cap bit per tool. Each has an expiry and a budget of calls.
- **Calls:**
  1. The agent proves and executes `reserve(tool, requestId)` through the gate. The target `ServiceEscrow` records the reservation; the budget is spent atomically with the proof.
  2. A local HTTP gateway (node `http`, localhost) serves the request only if it sees the reservation event.
  3. Afterwards it posts `commit(requestId, resultHash)` or `fail(requestId)` (operator transaction).
- **Off-chain outcomes are not atomic with the on-chain spend.** The gateway fails with probability 0.1 per request (→ `fail`). With probability 0.05 it crashes after serving and before commit; the reservation stays open and is reported as **uncertain**.
- **Trace events** (seeded):
  - normal calls;
  - a permission change mid-trace: one agent loses `fetch`, through a new certificate and revocation of the old edge;
  - a revocation of one agent;
  - 2 concurrent calls by one agent from one note (expected: one BudgetSpent, then a retry);
  - expiry of one agent's certificate in the middle of the trace;
  - known over-authorization attempts: a tool without its cap bit (`admin`), a call after revocation, a call after expiry, a call beyond budget.

**A2 — sandbox assets (test ERC-20 vault).**
- A treasury T funds a `Vault` that holds a test ERC-20. T delegates to 3 agents with spend budgets. Methods `pay` (cap bit 0) and `approve` (cap bit 1); `approve` is granted to one agent only.
- `Vault.onAuthorized(method, amount, recipient)` transfers or approves tokens. The proof, the budget spend and the token transfer happen in **one transaction**: a revert anywhere rolls back everything.
- **Trace events:**
  - payments to seeded recipients;
  - a payment that the vault cannot cover (vault balance too low → the target reverts → the whole transaction reverts, the nullifier stays unspent, and the agent can retry after a top-up);
  - revocation of one agent;
  - over-authorization attempts: `approve` without its bit, an amount above the remaining budget, a replay of a used proof, a call after revocation.

**A3 — multi-agent orchestration (bounded procurement).**
- A planner P (one hop below the operator) splits a task into N = 24 purchases across 4–8 sub-agents (seeded), with a total budget B and per-category caps. Three categories, three methods / cap bits; each sub-agent gets 1–2 categories. P delegates budget notes to the sub-agents (real transfers).
- Sub-agents buy through `Procurement.onAuthorized(categoryMethod, price, itemId)`.
- **Injected faults** (seeded):
  - a sub-agent goes offline (its items are reassigned by P);
  - P revokes one misbehaving sub-agent and reassigns;
  - transient gateway errors → retry;
  - known over-authorization requests: a category outside the sub-agent's cap, a purchase above its remaining budget, a purchase after revocation, re-use of another sub-agent's note.
- **Task success** = all N items bought, total spend ≤ B, every purchase in an allowed category.

## Trace generation
- `gen_traces.mjs <app> <seed>` writes `traces/<app>/<seed>.json`: the event list with logical timestamps.
- 30 seeds per application, generated and **committed before any harness run**. The distributions (counts, probabilities) are fixed in the generator's header.

## Metrics (per run, per application)
- **Authorization safety (hard):**
  - every known over-authorization attempt is blocked;
  - no action is accepted outside its certificate's cap, expiry or budget, or after revocation plus Δ;
  - total spend ≤ budget.

  Each blocked attempt is recorded with its **blocking layer**: (1) no satisfiable witness (circuit), (2) gate revert (with reason), or (3) target revert.
- **Task success rate:**
  - A3: all items within budget and caps;
  - A1: the share of intended legitimate calls that ended in commit;
  - A2: the share of intended legitimate payments that settled.
- **Off-chain uncertainty (A1):** counts of fail, uncertain and commit.
- **Atomicity (A2):** in reverted vault calls, the budget nullifier is unspent and no token moved.
- **Failure trajectories:** every failed, blocked or uncertain event is kept with its full record (`results/<app>/<seed>.json`).
- **Latency decomposition (research design E4, nine stages):**
  - credential issuance;
  - revocation-path fetch;
  - witness assembly;
  - witness calculation;
  - proving;
  - off-chain verification;
  - submission;
  - finality (inclusion; anvil, not a real chain);
  - action execution (the target's effect, read back).

  **Measured on the Mac M5 Pro only, uncontended**: seed 0 of each application, run alone. The 30-seed functional runs may run on RTX6000; their wall times are not reported.

## Statistics
- Descriptive. Rates with Clopper–Pearson 95 % CIs over the runs.
- Blocking is a hard check: any accepted over-authorization attempt is a **finding**.
- No hypothesis test.
